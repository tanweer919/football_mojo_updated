Mbappé's first World Cup as captain — France's high-wire act in New Jersey.

MW
Marcus Wright
SENIOR WRITER · UPDATED 14 MAY · 6 MIN
Subscriber

In a tunnel beneath MetLife Stadium last week, Kylian Mbappé stood with the captain's armband in his hand and weighed it like it were heavier than the trophy it was meant to deliver. Twenty-eight days from now, France will run out for their World Cup opener — and for the first time in his career, the 27-year-old will lead them.

Didier Deschamps's decision, made quietly in February and confirmed without ceremony at Clairefontaine, was the worst-kept secret in French football. Antoine Griezmann had retired from the national team in November. Hugo Lloris's gloves had already been hung up in Los Angeles. The succession was clear; the only question was whether Mbappé wanted it.

He did. He always had.

You don't ask Kylian to lead. He arrives leading, whether the room knows it yet or not. — Didier Deschamps, France HC

The high wire he now walks runs from Pasadena (group stage) to Atlanta (likely R16), then potentially to Dallas, to Foxborough, and finally, on 19 July, to the stadium in East Rutherford where Pelé played his last competitive match. France are seeded second behind Argentina. The bracket is kind for the first two rounds and brutal thereafter.

What's changed in the year since Mbappé's transfer to Madrid is harder to name than to feel. The boy who scored a Ligue 1 hat-trick at 17 is no longer the protégé of anyone. At Real, in the spring's biggest games, he has begun to drop deeper, dictate tempo, talk back. Once he was the gift; now, increasingly, he is the giver.

Whether that translates in a tournament he has approached three times and won once remains the question Deschamps cannot answer in May. It will answer itself in July — under floodlights, in front of cameras, in front of a country that has waited four years to be told who, exactly, France are.
